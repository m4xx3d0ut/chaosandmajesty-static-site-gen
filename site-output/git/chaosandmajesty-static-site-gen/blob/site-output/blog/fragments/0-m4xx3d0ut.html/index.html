  26. <h2>The Beginning (No Preconception)</h2>
  27. <p>Sometimes to get a point across you need some background, sometimes you have to go back to the beginning (learning from the past). <strong>I come from a different time and place, &quot;We&quot; all do. We are called by many names, but &quot;We&quot; are &quot;Hackers&quot;.</strong> It was the beginning, one could say that we evolved alongside the &quot;home computer&quot;, the advent of the internet (the &quot;wild west&quot; as it were), and when &quot;phreaking&quot; was still a viable skill (not sorry MaBell). Our relationship with technology was, and still is, symbiotic. You wouldn&#39;t recognize the name of my first system&#39;s OS, but you&#39;ve interacted with it countless times. You&#39;ve interacted with it&#39;s successor, in all likelihood, recently. Understanding these systems as they evolved (Win, Mac, *Nix, Systems Architecture, Cloud, etc.) was not about the superficial &quot;user level&quot; interactions taught in school, academia as a whole failed to serve our needs, it <em>meant more</em> to us. We set out to seek the truth, the strongest of us found it and showed the rest the way. It was a hive mind in a sense, the internet was still the wild west, InfoSec was not a formal concept, the technology was young, and we shared knowledge through all open channels. It became part of us, the collective knowledge that flowed through us and &quot;augmented&quot; the capabilities of our (now ancient) systems. This was formative, we learned more than systems architecture and programming, we learned &quot;survival traits&quot;, &quot;life hacks&quot;, and community.</p>
  28. <p>Those years gave birth to methodologies and practices. <strong>We learned to exceed our peers by simply &quot;applying our art&quot;.</strong> We learned to teach identify and teach ardent students, our numbers grow. For once you fully understand a system, any system (software, hardware, social, economic, political, etc.), you are in a favorable position. This is where novel ideas and exploits exist (they are one in the same), the space in between known and the void, the ether. It is through the pursuit of knowledge that we are able to navigate perpetual liminality, the quiet frontier of thought, where time looses meaning, and the self ceases to exist. In the stark silence, you will begin to hear. In the dark of the void, you will begin to see the path.</p>
  29. <h2>The Now</h2>
  30. <p>AI, as we know it today, has created opportunity for us in many ways. The idle fear mongering around the technology is best left to the uninitiated. You must see it for what it is, a system, and an enabling technology. This is an era defining moment in some ways, we have come full circle. Where our knowledge once &quot;augmented&quot; the capabilities of our systems, our systems now &quot;augment&quot; us. Utilized properly one can learn, research, prototype, code, debug, and harden systems faster alone than we could with a team five years ago. In the hands of the adept, it becomes an unparalleled force multiplier. There is a common misconception that AI will replace &quot;coders&quot;, in a sense there is some truth to this but I feel the statement is largely misleading. The truth of this statement only exists under the context of the current dev team. Take a moment to consider your resources, a Lead Dev, Sr. Dev, <em>N</em> number of Jr. Devs, Sr. / Jr. DevOps / DevSecOps (embedded or otherwise). This may extend further to Systems / Cloud Architects, Sr. / Jr. Kubernetes Specialists, Sr. / Jr.Database Engineers, and the list goes on. This is a wide spectrum of skills, experience, languages, frameworks, and technologies. Until recently and still in most cases, to operate at scale, you need the full spectrum, however as the technology landscape changes so will resource needs. Allow me to share with you my vision of the evolving landscape, where the skill shift is likely to occur, and what the impact on the near-term tech team structure / resources may be.</p>
  31. <h2>The Future (Starting Now... because &quot;The Now&quot; is now the past...)</h2>
  32. <p>Let&#39;s discuss direct tech team resource impact first, there are roles that will / have become deprecated. To no surprise, Jr. roles as we know them today will become obsolete, the type of tasks this role typically handles are well within the wheel house of modern AI systems. Specific verticals may feel the effect near-term as well, Sr. / Jr. &quot;Web Dev&quot; roles dealing primarily with front-end / UI and the more Jr. DB Engineering roles will be impacted for the same reasons. Many roles that deal with boiler plate code, data processing, bug triage, and basic feature work will fall to the same sword.</p>
  33. <p>Now, reconsider your resource needs and factor in a plan to up-skill any remaining / Sr. roles to integrate <em>effective</em> AI tooling into their workflows. What do I mean by effective AI tooling? This is not a one size fits all solution, in many cases this may require your team to analyze their workflow and develop in-house tooling. I prefer to work from Neovim, heavily customized as a full IDE, I&#39;ve built custom plugins specific to my workflow, and written plugins to extend the capabilities of other plugins to &quot;optimize&quot; my workflow. Combined with ample public / private cloud resources and a zero-trust proxy, I have cultivated a hybrid workflow leaning heavily into GitOps / LlmOps methodologies allowing me to work full-stack with great speed and dramatically reduced effort. Granted that I am a Cloud Architect and Principal Technologist, my level of technical skill is high, but not without gaps. This workflow has not bolstered weaker skills, but drastically increased the efficiency of stronger skills.</p>
  34. <p>This result is not special, any high-level engineer willing to take the time to implement their own AI workflow can achieve similar results. Why? Allow me to make some (potentially strong) statements about a skilled engineer applying similar methodology to their workflow; all programming languages are &quot;formal&quot; and paradigms of one language often apply to others, approaching a stack with at least base knowledge of all systems can quickly extrapolate to full working knowledge, specialists see massive efficiency boosts in their respective verticals, working with complex schema based or declarative systems become simple. I am not implying that a single Sr. Dev can add AI to their workflow and immediately begin producing cloud-native production ready systems, but depending on your needs and desired scale a single Architect might. I&#39;ll leave the math to you, but I feel confident it will agree. You still need general developer and specialist roles on your team, but less human capital. In short, you are condensing roles relative to your scale (already typical) but focusing on acquiring smaller numbers of high-level talent who augment their abilities with workflow specific AI tooling. A small dev team, where your expectation would perhaps be 5-10 roles may be reduced as low as 1-2 in some cases. Once again this is not one size fits all and the need of each project / company will vary, but the same principal will apply.</p>
  35. <p>Now to discuss the skill shift and quell any remaining fear mongering around the technology (other than AI weapons systems and &quot;battlefield convergence&quot; doctrine). The skill shift must occur at the lower level (not really a tech job but most people consider it a tech role) and entry level (true) tech roles. Jobs will become obsolete as a result of the evolution of AI, but those displaced can be up-skilled or re-skilled into new roles as result of the same evolution. Course curriculum&#39;s, I hesitate to will need to adapt to the new landscape, assuring that graduates are confident in their ability to leverage AI as a tool, effectively, in their workflows. The result, a renewed talent pool with greatly enhanced technical skills and a massive surge in the industry as further advances in AI continue to extend the capabilities of skilled humans. I don&#39;t feel that we should expect AI to take opportunity, but to create opportunities yet unknown to us. Welcome to the liminal space, in between the known and the void, I&#39;ve been here my entire life and I&#39;m happy to show you the way.</p>
  36. <p>Are we in the midst of the AI bubble, yes we are. It is how we proceed through the next stages of the technologies evolution that will determine the long-term viability and practical use these systems in our day-to-day lives. Believing the hype that any AI system currently on the market can fully replace a human employee is not only a dream, but will contribute to the impact the economy will feel if (when) that bubble bursts. What each and every executive, product, and engineering lead should be considering right now is the responsible of these technologies in their workflows and day-to-day business operations. Else, it won&#39;t be AI coming to take your job, it will be the executive, product, or engineering lead that knows how to use AI coming to eat your lunch.</p>
